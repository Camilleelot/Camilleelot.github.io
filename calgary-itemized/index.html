The proposed 2027 budget, read like a household budget

Made by Camille from the City of Calgary’s published numbers, as of November 22, 2026. Not an official City page.

On November 10 the City of Calgary proposed its budget for 2027 to 2030. It would raise property tax revenue 15% in 2027, then 10%, 7.7% and 6%.City of Calgary, proposed 2027–2030 Budget, and the Ward 8 budget toolkit. The 15% is revenue from properties that already exist. For the typical house, the City’s part of the property tax bill goes from $229 a month to $266, and on to about $334 by 2030 .The typical house is the City’s median single-residential assessment: $706,000 in 2026 and $688,000 in 2027. The years after 2027 are this page’s projection. Council hears from the public starting November 23 and votes in December.

A city budget is too big to picture. A household budget isn’t. So this page shows your share of the City: by the month, line by line, for your own home.

Each point of increase costs your home about $2.31 a month and raises about $28 million citywide.“Each roughly $28 million in added budget cost represents a one per cent property tax increase.” LiveWire Calgary, September 4, 2026. Holding 2027 to 10% instead would save your home about $11.57 a month and leave about $140 million less to spend.

And 41.5% of every property tax bill isn’t the City’s at all. It’s the Province’s school tax: $1,948 a year on your home.

What happens next

  • November 10. The City released the proposal.
  • November 23. Public submissions begin and Council starts deliberating. Register to speak or send written comments at calgary.ca/budget.
  • December. Council votes on the budget, with any changes it makes.
  • Spring 2027. After the provincial budget, Council sets the 2027 tax rates.

How the numbers are made

  1. 2026 City tax is your 2026 assessed value times 0.0038906, the City’s 2026 residential rate (rates).
  2. The City puts the typical house at $266 a month in 2027 on a $688,000 assessment. That implies a 2027 rate of about 0.0046395 under the 15% proposal; your 2027 tax is your 2027 value times that rate.
  3. The slider runs from 0% to 30% and scales the rate by (1 + your increase) ÷ 1.15. At 15% the City’s figures put the typical house up 16.2%, because values and tax shares shift between years; the slider keeps that shift, so even 0% shows a small rise.
  4. With no 2027 value of your own, yours moves like the typical house’s, down 2.5%. The typical condo falls 5.6%, from $347,000 to $327,500 (CTV News, November 10, 2026).
  5. 2028 to 2030 apply the proposed 10%, 7.7% and 6% to the rate, with your value held flat.
  6. Yours, a year: your City tax split by the City’s 2026 tax breakdown and matched to each budget line. The option captions use July’s City estimates as reported by Global News (5.4% to keep services, 20.2% and about $510 million for every request) and about $28 million per point.
  7. Inside the subscriptions: your police line is split 85 to 15, the way the service describes its budget; the running-the-City lines are the City’s own; transit’s cents are citywide, from the 2026 budget.
  8. The ledger leaves out water, waste and recycling charges and the Province’s school tax. The whole-bill chart adds them back: water at the City’s typical-home estimate, and your school tax at the 2026 provincial rate, scaled for 2027 the way the City scales the typical house.
  9. Shares of income use the household income you enter; the default is Calgary’s 2020 median, $98,000.

Every figure links to its source. Calgary, Itemized is independent: it isn’t made, checked or endorsed by the City of Calgary.
