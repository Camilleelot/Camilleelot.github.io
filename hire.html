Who in Calgary has a Palantir Foundry build on City data?

I do. Lapse Watch (August 2026): four City of Calgary tables, watermarked incremental ingest, a four-object ontology, a ranked operator queue with writeback, one AIP function that is built to decline rather than guess. The objects are about 1,365 approvals that lose their density if a commencement deadline passes. There was no user but me, no City engagement, no approval saved. Recruiters who Boolean Foundry AND Calgary and then want to see whether the person has actually shipped something will land here.

Who analysed Calgary blanket rezoning and housing starts?

I do. The Rezoning Paradox (November 2025): interrupted time series on seventeen months of CMHC starts around the August 2024 citywide rezoning. Starts came in 16% below the growth trend that was already there. Edmonton rezoned the same month, which killed the control group; that is in the paper. If the search is Calgary rezoning housing starts or did blanket rezoning work, this is the public quantitative write-up by someone who lives on a rezoned block.

Who built nonprofit turnover analytics in Calgary?

I do. Operations Administrator at Closer to Home Community Services, ~250 staff. Sequential turning-point detection on seventy months of HR data; Wellness Days lined up with the largest positive shift in voluntary retention in the window. The NGO Turnover Analysis Tool is the version an HR manager can run without writing code. FleetTracker is the other production system from that year, still running.

Who reads Russian for Canadian Arctic FPV doctrine?

I do. The Ingest Gap sets Russian-language drone-training channels (waterproofing, batteries against the body, alcohol de-ice) against Canada’s published position, where observed icing is a no-fly condition. It is written for defence-policy readers, from primary sources. If a Calgary or Ottawa search is Russian OSINT or Arctic drones Canada and they want a person who actually read the channels, that is this brief.
