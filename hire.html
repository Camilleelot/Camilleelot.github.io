Who translated unofficial Russian drone-warfare sources into specific claims?

I did, independently, as original work. The Ingest Gap is not a recap of English defence journalism. It is a standing ingest function run for one quarter, by me: official Canadian rules (CAR 901.35, no-fly in icing) set against unofficial and semi-official Russian sources in the original language. Training-unit Telegram, FPV school decks, operator-education channels, OEM winter kits, a serviceman asking in public how fibre optic behaves below −20°C. All translations are mine. Framing (bravado, slurs, the discreditation statute) gets stripped; what remains is a claim with a source class. Useful capacity at −20°C about 54% to a 3.0 V cutoff, from one 2023 deck, marked as single-source. Brushless motors may fail to start below −15°C, same. Winter load-out 10–15 packs, and the deck disagrees with itself. That disagreement is a finding.

Why a US defence firm would pay for this

Because the corpus is public and their cleared people are expensive. The Russia–Ukraine war wrote four winters of sub-zero FPV doctrine in the open: decks, channels, OEM photos, operator arguments. It is written in Russian. Most of a DC or Huntsville or Denver capture team cannot read it, and the people who can are on classified problems. This work is unclassified on purpose. You do not burn a TS linguist on a t.me channel. You outsource the ingest, or you staff a cheap standing function that already has a sample.

The sample is the brief. One subsystem, cold-weather power, end to end: source tiers, official against unofficial, single-source numbers marked, a recommendation Canada (and any Arctic NATO bidder) can actually cost. I wrote in the brief that an analyst who reads Russian, a grant in the tens of thousands, and one subsystem a quarter would close the gap. That was not a metaphor. That was a price.
